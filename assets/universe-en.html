2.2Yav & Nav
Yav

The world of the living. The world of men, courts, wars and intrigue.

Spawn of Yav

Mavkas, wolf-walkers, the hollowed and other lesser fiends are spawn of Yav itself. Former people with the Nav mixed into them. Creatures in between, not true dwellers of the Nav.

Nav

The world beyond Yav — with laws and creatures of its own.

Creatures of the Nav

Cracklers, bone-gnawers, bog-dwellers — creatures that slipped into Yav through wormholes from the upper layer of the Nav.

2.3Orochit & the Safe Lands

The key to a safe world is orochit stone. No fiend can pass it.

In Lovarra orochit is mined and built into walls: a belt around every village, stone in the masonry of the cities. That is why the South is safe — no reapers are needed there, people live in peace behind high walls, and Lovarran women do not fear the fiends. Coffins are reinforced with it too: in the South the dead are not burned as in the North but sealed in coffins of orochit.

Thornhall is the exact opposite. Wild, dangerous lands swarming with fiends, where reapers are expendable material.

Section III

Reapers

People reforged with the blood of the Nav — a weapon against the fiends.

At the core of a reaper lies ichtor, black blood: stabilised blood of the Nav poured into a human during the reforging. Ichtor is thicker than human blood, and so a reaper’s heart beats slowly and rarely. That same blood grants strength and endurance beyond ordinary men, accelerated healing of wounds, and dulls the body: reapers feel neither pain nor cold.

A reaper’s strength is finite and bounded by the “Reserve” — the concentration of Nav blood, held stable by the dusk potion. That proportion is unstable: the black blood strives to subdue what remains of the red. If this critical limit is crossed in the heat of battle, an irreversible reaction begins. The reaper’s mind burns away in the Nav, and his body becomes an empty, predatory shell.

The standing of reapers in society — in the ledgers they are listed as “property of the Citadel”, above horses and bone-gnawers.
